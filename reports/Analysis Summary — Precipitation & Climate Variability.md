# 📊 Analysis Summary — Precipitation & Climate Variability

This analysis focuses on understanding precipitation patterns in Atlantic Canada, including trends, variability, and relationships with other environmental indicators.

---

## Key Findings

- Precipitation shows a clear increasing trend over time  
- Monthly variability is high (~57%), indicating unstable patterns  
- Extreme weather events have become more frequent  

---

## Relationships Across Variables

- Weak correlation between temperature and precipitation  
- No consistent relationship between precipitation and GHG emissions  
- Regional patterns vary significantly across provinces  

---

## Interpretation

These results indicate that climate behavior is becoming more complex and less predictable.  

Precipitation patterns do not follow a single driving factor and must be interpreted within a broader multi-variable context.

---

## Conclusion

Precipitation in Atlantic Canada is increasing, more variable, and more extreme.  

However, these changes are not directly explained by other environmental indicators, highlighting the need for integrated climate analysis.
