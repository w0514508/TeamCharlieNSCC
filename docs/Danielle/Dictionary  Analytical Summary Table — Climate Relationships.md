## 🧩 Analytical Summary Table — Climate Relationships

### Overview

This table was manually created within Power BI to summarize key insights across provinces, integrating results from precipitation, temperature, air quality, extreme events, and GHG analysis.

It does not represent raw data, but rather a **derived interpretation layer** designed to support storytelling and decision-making.

---

### Source

- Created manually in Power BI
- Based on interpretation of multiple datasets:
  - Precipitation metrics
  - Temperature indicators
  - Extreme event frequencies
  - GHG emissions trends
  - Air quality indicators

---

### Purpose

- Provide a **high-level comparative view** across provinces
- Summarize **multi-variable relationships** in a simplified format
- Support communication of complex analytical findings
- Highlight absence of consistent relationships across environmental indicators

---

### Grain

- Province-level

---

### Fields Description

#### `Province`
- Geographic identifier for analysis.

#### `AQI`
- Qualitative classification of air quality levels.
- Derived from AQI analysis (Low / Moderate / High).

#### `Temp`
- Relative temperature level classification.
- Based on normalized or indexed temperature measures.

#### `Precip`
- Relative precipitation level classification.
- Derived from precipitation intensity and averages.

#### `Extreme`
- Level of extreme weather events.
- Based on annual frequency and trends.

#### `GHG`
- Emissions trend classification.
- Indicates whether emissions are increasing, stable, or decreasing.

#### `Insight`
- Summary interpretation of relationships between variables.
- In this case, highlights that no consistent relationship exists across environmental indicators.

---

### Key Insight

The table shows that, despite variations in temperature, precipitation, extreme events, and emissions, **no consistent or direct relationship can be identified across provinces**.

---

### Notes

- This table is **not used for calculations**
- It is used exclusively for:
  - Storytelling

- Classifications are:
  - Relative (not absolute)
  - Based on observed patterns in the data

- Subject to interpretation bias
